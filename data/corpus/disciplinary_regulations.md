# Northbridge Institute of Technology — Disciplinary Regulations

**Document Code:** NIT/DISC/2024-25
**Effective Date:** August 1, 2024
**Approved By:** University Senate, Resolution No. 2024/SEN/045

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1. These regulations establish the framework for maintaining discipline and order at the Northbridge Institute of Technology (NIT). They apply to all enrolled students and cover conduct on campus, in hostels, during Institute-sponsored events, and in any context where the student is representing or associated with NIT.

1.2. The primary objective of the disciplinary framework is to create a safe, respectful, and conducive learning environment for all members of the NIT community. Disciplinary actions are intended to be corrective rather than punitive, except in cases of severe misconduct.

## 2. Categories of Misconduct

2.1. **Minor Misconduct** includes but is not limited to:
   a) Disruption of classes, examinations, or Institute events.
   b) Unauthorized absence from mandatory academic activities.
   c) Minor violations of hostel rules (noise, cleanliness, late return).
   d) Use of inappropriate language or disrespectful behaviour towards peers.
   e) Unauthorized use of Institute facilities or resources.
   f) Minor traffic and parking violations on campus.

2.2. **Major Misconduct** includes but is not limited to:
   a) Academic misconduct (plagiarism, cheating, fabrication of data).
   b) Harassment, bullying, or intimidation of any member of the NIT community.
   c) Sexual harassment or misconduct as defined by applicable law and Institute policy.
   d) Physical violence, threats of violence, or endangering the safety of others.
   e) Possession, consumption, or distribution of alcohol, tobacco, or illegal substances on campus.
   f) Theft, vandalism, or willful destruction of Institute or personal property.
   g) Proxy attendance or impersonation in examinations.
   h) Ragging in any form, as defined by the UGC Anti-Ragging Regulations.
   i) Unauthorized possession of weapons or explosive materials.
   j) Submitting fraudulent documents (medical certificates, income certificates, etc.).

2.3. **Criminal Misconduct** includes any action that constitutes a criminal offence under applicable law. Such cases shall be reported to law enforcement authorities in addition to Institute disciplinary proceedings.

## 3. Disciplinary Authorities

3.1. **Course Instructor:** Has the authority to deal with minor misconduct occurring within the context of their course. Corrective actions may include verbal warning, written warning, or deduction of marks (up to 5% of the course grade).

3.2. **Head of Department:** Has the authority to deal with minor misconduct that is escalated by the course instructor or that involves students within the department. May impose fines up to ₹2,000 or recommend more severe action.

3.3. **Warden:** Has the authority to deal with hostel-related misconduct. May impose fines, restrict hostel privileges, or recommend suspension from hostel accommodation.

3.4. **Disciplinary Committee:** The primary adjudicating body for all cases of major misconduct. The Committee comprises the Dean of Student Affairs (Chairperson), two senior faculty members, one representative from the legal cell, and one student representative from the Student Council.

3.5. **Vice-Chancellor:** The final appellate authority for all disciplinary matters. The Vice-Chancellor may also initiate disciplinary proceedings in cases of institutional significance.

## 4. Disciplinary Procedure

4.1. Any member of the NIT community (student, faculty, or staff) may report an alleged act of misconduct by submitting a written complaint to the relevant disciplinary authority or through the online complaint portal on the NIT website.

4.2. Upon receipt of a complaint, the disciplinary authority shall conduct a preliminary inquiry within five (5) working days to determine whether the complaint has merit. Anonymous complaints shall be accepted but must contain sufficient detail for investigation.

4.3. If the preliminary inquiry suggests that the complaint has merit, the accused student shall be served a written notice of the allegations, along with the evidence available. The student shall be given at least seven (7) working days to submit a written response.

4.4. For cases of major misconduct, a formal hearing shall be conducted by the Disciplinary Committee. The accused student has the right to:
   a) Be informed of the specific allegations and evidence against them.
   b) Present their defence, including witnesses and documentary evidence.
   c) Be accompanied by a fellow student or a faculty advisor (but not a legal representative) during the hearing.
   d) Receive a written record of the hearing proceedings.

4.5. The Disciplinary Committee shall reach its decision by a majority vote. The decision, along with the reasoning, shall be communicated to the student in writing within ten (10) working days of the conclusion of the hearing.

## 5. Penalties

5.1. The following penalties may be imposed, individually or in combination, depending on the nature and severity of the misconduct:

| Penalty | Description |
|---------|------------|
| Verbal Warning | Informal corrective action for first-time minor offences |
| Written Warning | Formal warning placed in the student's disciplinary file |
| Fine | Monetary penalty up to ₹10,000 |
| Community Service | Up to 40 hours of community service on campus |
| Suspension from Hostel | Temporary or permanent removal from hostel accommodation |
| Academic Penalty | Grade reduction or "F" grade in the affected course |
| Suspension | Exclusion from all academic activities for up to two semesters |
| Expulsion | Permanent removal from the Institute with no eligibility for readmission |
| Degree Revocation | Withdrawal of a conferred degree if misconduct is discovered post-graduation |

5.2. Multiple penalties may be applied concurrently. For example, a student found guilty of examination cheating may receive both an "F" grade in the course and a one-semester suspension.

5.3. A record of all disciplinary actions (except verbal warnings) is maintained in the student's permanent file and may be referenced in future disciplinary proceedings, scholarship reviews, or character certificate requests.

## 6. Anti-Ragging Provisions

6.1. Ragging, in any form, is strictly prohibited at NIT. Ragging includes any act that causes physical or psychological harm, humiliation, or harassment to a student, particularly a junior student, by another student or group of students.

6.2. NIT has constituted an Anti-Ragging Committee and an Anti-Ragging Squad in compliance with UGC Regulations. Any incident of ragging shall be dealt with swiftly and severely.

6.3. Penalties for ragging include: immediate suspension pending investigation; expulsion from the hostel; rustication for up to four semesters; permanent expulsion; and reporting to law enforcement authorities.

6.4. An anti-ragging helpline (toll-free) is available 24/7. The helpline number is displayed prominently in all hostel blocks, academic buildings, and the NIT website.

## 7. Appeals

7.1. A student who has been penalized may appeal to the Vice-Chancellor within fifteen (15) working days of receiving the penalty notification. The appeal must be submitted in writing and must clearly state the grounds for appeal.

7.2. Grounds for appeal include: new evidence not available at the time of the original hearing; procedural irregularities in the disciplinary process; disproportionate penalty relative to the offence; or a material error in the interpretation of the relevant regulations.

7.3. The Vice-Chancellor may uphold the original decision, modify the penalty, or order a rehearing. The Vice-Chancellor's decision is final and binding.

## 8. Interim Measures

8.1. In cases where the alleged misconduct poses an immediate threat to the safety of individuals or the integrity of an ongoing examination, the Dean of Student Affairs may impose interim measures pending the outcome of the disciplinary process. These may include temporary suspension from classes, hostel, or the campus.

8.2. Interim measures are not penalties and do not prejudice the outcome of the disciplinary process. If the student is ultimately found not guilty, any academic or financial consequences of the interim measures shall be remedied.

## 9. Record Keeping and Confidentiality

9.1. All disciplinary records are maintained by the Office of the Dean of Student Affairs. Records are retained for a minimum of seven (7) years after the student's graduation or departure from the Institute.

9.2. Disciplinary proceedings are conducted confidentially. Information about ongoing or completed proceedings shall not be disclosed to unauthorized parties.

## 10. Amendments

10.1. These regulations may be amended by the University Senate at any time. Amendments shall take effect thirty (30) days after publication on the NIT website.
