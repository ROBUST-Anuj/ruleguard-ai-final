# Northbridge Institute of Technology — Medical Exemption Policy

**Document Code:** NIT/MED/2024-25
**Effective Date:** August 1, 2024
**Approved By:** Dean of Academic Affairs and Director, NIT Health Centre

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1. This policy establishes the framework for medical exemptions and accommodations available to students at the Northbridge Institute of Technology (NIT) who experience health-related challenges that affect their ability to meet standard academic requirements.

1.2. This policy covers exemptions related to attendance requirements, examination participation, assignment deadlines, and other academic obligations that may be impacted by a student's health condition. It applies to both acute medical conditions (illness, injury, surgery) and chronic health conditions that require ongoing management.

1.3. This policy is administered jointly by the NIT Health Centre and the Dean of Academic Affairs. All medical documentation is treated as confidential and is stored in accordance with the Institute's data protection guidelines.

## 2. Eligibility

2.1. Any currently enrolled student who experiences a medical condition that significantly impairs their ability to attend classes, complete assessments, or sit for examinations may apply for a medical exemption.

2.2. The following conditions are generally recognized as grounds for a medical exemption:

   a) Hospitalization for a period of three (3) or more consecutive days.
   b) Surgical procedures requiring a recovery period certified by a medical professional.
   c) Communicable diseases where attending classes would pose a public health risk.
   d) Chronic conditions with documented acute flare-ups (e.g., severe asthma, epilepsy, autoimmune disorders).
   e) Mental health conditions diagnosed and documented by a licensed psychiatrist or clinical psychologist.
   f) Maternity-related medical needs.

2.3. Minor ailments such as common cold, mild headache, or routine dental visits shall not ordinarily qualify for a medical exemption. The NIT Health Centre retains the discretion to evaluate each case individually.

## 3. Medical Exemption for Attendance

3.1. Students who submit an approved medical exemption certificate from the NIT Health Centre or a recognized hospital may be permitted to appear for semester-end examinations with attendance as low as 60%, subject to approval by the Dean of Academic Affairs.

3.2. The medical certificate must clearly state the diagnosis (or a general category of the condition, if the student requests confidentiality), the period of incapacity, and the treating physician's recommendation regarding the student's ability to resume academic activities.

3.3. Medical certificates must be submitted to the NIT Health Centre within five (5) working days of the student's return to academic activities. Late submissions shall be considered only in exceptional circumstances and at the discretion of the Dean of Academic Affairs.

3.4. The NIT Health Centre may require the student to undergo a verification examination by a panel physician before approving the exemption. This is a routine verification measure and does not imply any doubt regarding the student's medical condition.

3.5. Upon approval, the relevant sessions during the documented period of incapacity shall be excluded from the attendance percentage calculation. However, the attendance relaxation under this section shall not reduce the effective attendance requirement below 60%.

3.6. Students who have been granted a medical exemption but whose attendance still falls below 60% even after the exemption adjustment shall be debarred from the examination and will receive an "FA" grade.

## 4. Medical Exemption for Examinations

4.1. A student who is unable to attend a scheduled examination due to a medical emergency may apply for a make-up examination. The provisions of the Examination Policy (Section 8) regarding make-up examinations shall apply.

4.2. The medical certificate for an examination absence must be submitted within three (3) working days of the missed examination.

4.3. If a student falls ill during an ongoing examination and is unable to complete it, the invigilator shall note the circumstances and the student shall be permitted to apply for a make-up examination for the remaining portion of the assessment.

4.4. A student who is hospitalized during the entire examination period may apply for deferred examinations, which shall be scheduled in consultation with the Controller of Examinations and the student's treating physician.

## 5. Medical Leave of Absence

5.1. A student who requires an extended period of absence (more than four consecutive weeks) due to a medical condition may apply for a Medical Leave of Absence (MLOA). An MLOA allows the student to withdraw from the current semester without academic penalty.

5.2. A student on MLOA shall receive "W" (Withdrawn) grades for all courses in that semester. These grades do not affect the GPA calculation.

5.3. The MLOA must be supported by comprehensive medical documentation and is subject to approval by the Dean of Academic Affairs. The maximum duration of a continuous MLOA is two (2) semesters.

5.4. Before returning from an MLOA, the student must obtain a fitness certificate from the NIT Health Centre or submit a clearance letter from their treating physician.

## 6. Accommodations for Chronic Conditions

6.1. Students with documented chronic health conditions that require ongoing management may apply for semester-long accommodations. These may include:

   a) Permission to leave the classroom for short breaks without being marked absent.
   b) Flexible deadlines for assignments (up to a maximum extension of 72 hours).
   c) Special seating arrangements during examinations.
   d) Permission to bring medication or medical devices into the examination hall.

6.2. Applications for chronic condition accommodations must be submitted at the beginning of each semester, supported by updated medical documentation from the treating physician.

6.3. Chronic condition accommodations do not override the minimum attendance requirements specified in this policy (60% with medical exemption). Students whose chronic condition prevents them from meeting even the reduced attendance threshold should consider applying for a Medical Leave of Absence.

## 7. Confidentiality

7.1. All medical information submitted by students is treated as strictly confidential. Only authorized personnel in the NIT Health Centre, the Dean of Academic Affairs, and the relevant Head of Department shall have access to the student's medical records for the purpose of processing exemption requests.

7.2. Faculty members and course instructors shall be informed that a medical exemption has been granted, along with the applicable dates and accommodations, but shall not be given access to the underlying medical diagnosis unless the student provides explicit written consent.

## 8. Fraud and Misuse

8.1. Submission of fraudulent, forged, or misleading medical documentation constitutes a serious disciplinary offence and shall be dealt with under the Disciplinary Regulations.

8.2. Penalties for medical certificate fraud may include cancellation of the exemption, award of an "FA" grade, suspension for up to two semesters, or expulsion from the Institute.

## 9. Appeals

9.1. Students whose medical exemption applications are denied may appeal to the Vice-Chancellor's Office within ten (10) working days of receiving the denial notification. The appeal must include any additional medical documentation that supports the student's case.

9.2. The Vice-Chancellor's decision on the appeal shall be final and binding.

## 10. Amendments

10.1. This policy may be amended by the Dean of Academic Affairs in consultation with the Director of the NIT Health Centre, with the approval of the University Senate.
