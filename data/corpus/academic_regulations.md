# Northbridge Institute of Technology — Academic Regulations

**Document Code:** NIT/AR/2024-25
**Effective Date:** August 1, 2024
**Approved By:** University Senate, Resolution No. 2024/SEN/042

---

## 1. Preamble and Scope

1.1. The Northbridge Institute of Technology (NIT) Academic Regulations constitute the primary governance framework for all academic activities undertaken within the Institute. These regulations apply to all undergraduate, postgraduate, and doctoral students enrolled at NIT, as well as to all faculty members, teaching assistants, and administrative personnel involved in academic operations.

1.2. The Academic Regulations are reviewed and updated biennially by the University Senate. Any interim amendments must be approved by a two-thirds majority of the Senate and communicated to the student body at least thirty (30) days prior to their effective date.

1.3. In the event of a conflict between these regulations and any departmental guidelines, the provisions of this document shall prevail unless the departmental guidelines have been expressly approved by the Senate as an authorized exception.

1.4. All students are deemed to have read and accepted these regulations upon completion of their enrollment process. Ignorance of any provision contained herein shall not constitute a valid defence in any academic or disciplinary proceeding.

## 2. Program Structure and Duration

2.1. The standard duration for undergraduate Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech.) programs is four (4) academic years, comprising eight (8) regular semesters. The standard duration for Master of Technology (M.Tech.) programs is two (2) academic years, comprising four (4) regular semesters. The standard duration for the Doctor of Philosophy (Ph.D.) program is a minimum of three (3) academic years.

2.2. The maximum permissible duration for completion of the undergraduate program is six (6) academic years from the date of initial enrollment. For postgraduate programs, the maximum permissible duration is four (4) academic years. Extensions beyond these periods require exceptional approval from the Vice-Chancellor, supported by a recommendation from the Head of the Department and the Dean of Academic Affairs.

2.3. Each academic year is divided into an Autumn Semester (August to December), a Spring Semester (January to May), and an optional Summer Term (June to July). The Summer Term is available only for students who need to clear backlog courses or who wish to earn additional elective credits, subject to course availability.

2.4. A standard semester consists of a minimum of fourteen (14) weeks of instructional time, followed by a preparation week and an examination period of not less than two (2) weeks. The academic calendar is published by the Academic Affairs Office at least ninety (90) days prior to the commencement of each academic year.

2.5. Students who have exhausted the maximum permissible duration without completing their degree requirements shall be automatically struck off the rolls. Such students may apply for readmission under the provisions of the Readmission Policy, subject to the availability of seats and the approval of the Admissions Committee.

## 3. Course Registration

3.1. All students must register for their courses during the designated registration window at the beginning of each semester. The registration window is typically open for five (5) working days. Registration is conducted online through the NIT Student Information System (SIS).

3.2. A student may register for a maximum of twenty-four (24) credits and a minimum of twelve (12) credits per regular semester. Students who wish to register for more than twenty-four credits must obtain written approval from their Faculty Advisor and the Head of the Department.

3.3. Late registration is permitted up to five (5) working days after the close of the official registration window, subject to the payment of a late registration fee of ₹500 per day of delay. No registration shall be permitted after the late registration window.

3.4. Course withdrawal is permitted until the end of the sixth week of instruction. A withdrawn course will appear on the transcript with a grade of "W" (Withdrawn) and will not be counted in the GPA calculation. Withdrawal from all courses in a semester constitutes a semester withdrawal and requires approval from the Dean of Academic Affairs.

3.5. Students who fail to register for any semester without obtaining an approved leave of absence shall be deemed to have voluntarily withdrawn from the program.

## 4. Grading System

4.1. NIT employs a ten-point grading system. The letter grades, their corresponding grade points, and performance descriptors are as follows:

- **A+ (10 points):** Exceptional Performance
- **A (9 points):** Excellent Performance
- **B (8 points):** Very Good Performance
- **C (7 points):** Good Performance
- **D (6 points):** Fair Performance
- **E (5 points):** Satisfactory Performance
- **F (0 points):** Fail

4.2. The Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) is calculated as the credit-weighted average of grade points earned in a given semester. The Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) is the credit-weighted average of all grade points earned across all semesters.

4.3. A student must maintain a minimum CGPA of 5.0 to remain in good academic standing. A student whose CGPA falls below 5.0 at the end of any semester shall be placed on Academic Probation. The conditions and consequences of Academic Probation are specified in Section 10 of these regulations.

4.4. Grade moderation is conducted by the Course Instructor in consultation with the Head of the Department. Any appeal against an awarded grade must be submitted in writing to the Academic Affairs Office within ten (10) working days of the declaration of results.

4.5. The grade "I" (Incomplete) may be awarded when a student is unable to complete the course requirements due to documented medical or personal emergency. An Incomplete grade must be resolved within sixty (60) days of the end of the semester, failing which it shall be automatically converted to an "F" grade.

## 5. Supplementary Examinations

5.1. A student who receives an "F" grade in a course is eligible to apply for a supplementary examination for that course. Supplementary examinations are available for all courses, both core and elective.

5.2. Supplementary examinations are typically scheduled during the Summer Term or at the beginning of the next regular semester, as determined by the examination schedule published by the Controller of Examinations.

5.3. The maximum grade achievable in a supplementary examination is a "D" (6 grade points). This cap applies regardless of the student's actual performance in the supplementary examination.

5.4. Students who fail a supplementary examination may apply for a second supplementary attempt, provided they have a cumulative GPA of 5.0 or above and the course is a core/mandatory course. The second supplementary examination shall be conducted during the next regular examination cycle. The maximum grade achievable in the second supplementary attempt is also capped at "D" (6 grade points).

5.5. Applications for supplementary examinations must be submitted to the Controller of Examinations within two (2) weeks of the official declaration of regular semester results. Late applications shall not be entertained. The supplementary examination fee is ₹1,000 per course.

## 6. Fee Payment Guidelines

6.1. Registration for courses is provisional until all applicable tuition and administrative fees have been paid in full. A student who has not completed fee payment by the specified deadline may have their course registration cancelled.

6.2. The standard deadline for fee payment without penalty is announced at the start of each academic year. Students are notified of their fee obligations through the NIT Student Information System and via their official NIT email address.

6.3. Students may complete fee payment until September 20 without any academic penalty. Course registration shall remain valid during this grace period. Students who pay after September 20 shall be subject to late fee surcharges as specified in the Fee Regulations.

6.4. Students facing documented financial hardship may apply for a fee payment instalment plan through the Office of Student Financial Services. Such applications must be submitted before the standard fee deadline.

## 7. Academic Integrity

7.1. NIT maintains a zero-tolerance policy towards all forms of academic misconduct, including but not limited to: plagiarism, unauthorized collaboration on individually assessed work, cheating during examinations, fabrication or falsification of research data, and impersonation.

7.2. All suspected cases of academic misconduct shall be reported to the Disciplinary Committee for formal investigation. The accused student shall be given a reasonable opportunity to present their defence.

7.3. Penalties for academic misconduct may include, depending on severity: a formal written warning; a grade of "F" in the affected course; suspension from the program for up to two semesters; permanent expulsion from the Institute; or revocation of a degree if misconduct is discovered after graduation.

## 8. Graduation Requirements

8.1. To be eligible for the award of a degree, a student must have successfully completed all coursework prescribed for their program and earned the minimum total credits specified in the curriculum.

8.2. The student must have achieved a minimum CGPA of 5.0 and must not have any pending "F" or "I" grades on their transcript.

8.3. The student must have cleared all financial dues to the Institute, including tuition, hostel, library, and laboratory fees.

8.4. The student must have no pending disciplinary proceedings or sanctions at the time of the degree award.

8.5. Degree certificates are conferred at the Annual Convocation ceremony. Students who have completed their requirements between convocations may apply for a provisional degree certificate.

## 9. Academic Probation

9.1. A student shall be placed on Academic Probation if their CGPA falls below 5.0 at the end of any semester. Students on Academic Probation must meet with their Faculty Advisor at least once every two weeks to discuss their academic progress.

9.2. A student on Academic Probation may not register for more than eighteen (18) credits in a semester and may not hold any elected student body position.

9.3. If a student fails to raise their CGPA to 5.0 or above within two consecutive semesters of being placed on Academic Probation, they shall be required to appear before the Academic Review Board, which may recommend continued probation, reduced course load, or dismissal from the program.

## 10. Amendments

10.1. The University Senate reserves the right to amend these regulations at any time. All amendments shall be communicated to the student body via the official NIT website and email notification at least thirty (30) days prior to their effective date.

10.2. Amendments shall not be applied retroactively to adversely affect students who were enrolled prior to the date of the amendment, unless explicitly stated otherwise in the amendment resolution.
