# Northbridge Institute of Technology — Attendance Policy

**Document Code:** NIT/ATT/2024-25
**Effective Date:** August 1, 2024
**Approved By:** Dean of Academic Affairs

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1. This policy establishes the attendance requirements for all students enrolled in academic programs at the Northbridge Institute of Technology (NIT). Regular attendance is considered essential to the learning process and is a prerequisite for evaluation eligibility.

1.2. This policy applies to all undergraduate, postgraduate, and doctoral coursework. It covers attendance in lectures, tutorials, laboratory sessions, workshops, seminars, and any other scheduled academic activities that form part of the course curriculum.

1.3. Departments may establish additional attendance requirements for specific courses, such as laboratory-intensive programs, provided such requirements are communicated to students in the course syllabus at the beginning of the semester and do not reduce the minimum standards set by this policy.

## 2. Definition of Attendance

2.1. Attendance is defined as the physical presence of a student during a scheduled academic session, verified through the official attendance recording system. NIT uses a biometric attendance tracking system supplemented by manual verification by the course instructor.

2.2. A student shall be marked "present" only if they are physically present for at least 75% of the duration of a single class session. Arriving more than fifteen (15) minutes late to a one-hour session, or more than thirty (30) minutes late to a two-hour session, shall result in the student being marked absent for that session.

2.3. Proxy attendance (having another student register attendance on one's behalf) is a serious disciplinary offence and will be dealt with under the Disciplinary Regulations.

## 3. Recording and Monitoring

3.1. Course instructors are responsible for recording attendance at each scheduled session. Attendance records are uploaded to the NIT Student Information System (SIS) within forty-eight (48) hours of each session.

3.2. Students can view their attendance records in real-time through the SIS portal. Any discrepancy must be reported to the course instructor within five (5) working days of the session in question.

3.3. The Academic Affairs Office generates attendance status reports at the end of the sixth week, tenth week, and fourteenth week of each semester. Students whose attendance falls below 80% at the sixth-week checkpoint will receive a formal warning notification.

3.4. Heads of Departments shall review attendance data monthly and may convene meetings with students whose attendance is consistently below the minimum threshold.

## 4. Minimum Attendance Requirements

4.1. The minimum attendance requirement at NIT is computed on a course-by-course basis. Attendance percentages are calculated as the ratio of sessions attended to total sessions held for each course.

4.2. Students must maintain a minimum attendance of 75% in each registered course to be eligible for appearing in the semester-end examinations. No exceptions shall be granted below this threshold.

4.3. Students whose attendance in any course falls below 75% at the end of the semester shall be debarred from appearing in the semester-end examination for that course. Such students will receive an "FA" (Fail due to Attendance shortage) grade, which carries zero grade points and is counted in the GPA calculation.

4.4. The 75% minimum attendance requirement is calculated based on the total number of sessions scheduled for the course from the official start of instruction until the last day of instruction, excluding examination periods.

4.5. National holidays, Institute holidays, and days on which classes are officially suspended by the administration are excluded from the total session count for attendance calculation purposes.

## 5. Approved Absences

5.1. The following categories of absence may be considered for approval and shall not count against a student's attendance record, subject to proper documentation:

   a) **Medical absence:** Absences due to illness or medical procedures, supported by a medical certificate from the NIT Health Centre or a recognized hospital. The medical certificate must be submitted within five (5) working days of the student's return to class.

   b) **Institute-sponsored activities:** Absences due to participation in NIT-sponsored academic conferences, inter-institutional competitions, sports events, or cultural festivals, with prior written approval from the Head of the Department and the Dean of Student Affairs.

   c) **Family bereavement:** Absence of up to five (5) working days due to the death of an immediate family member (parent, sibling, spouse, or child), supported by appropriate documentation.

5.2. Approved absences do not add to the "present" count; instead, the relevant sessions are excluded from the total sessions used in calculating the attendance percentage.

5.3. A maximum of 15% of total sessions in any course may be excluded from the attendance calculation on account of approved absences. Beyond this limit, additional absences—even if documented—shall be counted as regular absences.

5.4. Applications for approval of absence must be submitted through the SIS portal along with supporting documentation. The course instructor and the Head of the Department shall jointly review and approve or deny such applications.

## 6. Consequences of Attendance Shortage

6.1. Students who are debarred from an examination due to attendance shortage shall receive an "FA" grade for the affected course. This grade is treated as equivalent to an "F" grade for all academic purposes, including GPA calculation, academic standing, and graduation eligibility.

6.2. A student who receives an "FA" grade must re-register for the course when it is next offered and meet all attendance and assessment requirements afresh.

6.3. If a student receives "FA" grades in three or more courses in a single semester, they shall be placed on Academic Probation as described in the Academic Regulations.

6.4. Repeated attendance shortages across multiple semesters may be referred to the Academic Review Board for consideration of more severe actions, including reduced course load requirements or suspension.

## 7. Attendance for Laboratory and Practical Courses

7.1. Laboratory and practical courses require a minimum attendance of 80%, which is higher than the standard 75% threshold. This higher requirement reflects the hands-on nature of these courses, where missed sessions cannot be adequately compensated through self-study.

7.2. Laboratory sessions missed due to approved absences must be completed through make-up sessions arranged by the course instructor. If make-up sessions cannot be arranged, the student's attendance shall be calculated based on the sessions actually attended versus the total scheduled sessions.

7.3. Students who fail to meet the 80% attendance requirement for a laboratory course shall receive an "FA" grade for that course, regardless of their performance in other assessments.

## 8. Attendance Relaxation for Special Categories

8.1. Students who are representing NIT at national or international academic, sports, or cultural events recognized by the Institute may be granted attendance relaxation of up to an additional 10% upon recommendation of the Dean of Student Affairs.

8.2. Students enrolled in the NIT Industry Internship Program during a regular semester shall be exempt from the attendance requirements for courses in which they have been granted a deferred examination.

8.3. Students with documented disabilities that affect their ability to attend classes regularly may apply for attendance accommodations through the Office of Disability Services. Such accommodations shall be determined on a case-by-case basis and communicated to all relevant course instructors.

## 9. Amendments

9.1. This policy may be amended by the Dean of Academic Affairs with the approval of the University Senate. Amendments shall be communicated to all students at least thirty (30) days before taking effect.
